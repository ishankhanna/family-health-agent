# Future product spec

What this could become: a **family health companion** that a household actually lives in — not a chatbot you open when someone is already unwell and close when the fever breaks.

A family should not rebuild its health history every time someone feels off. One thread for Aarav’s fever, Priya’s migraine, and Meera’s knees. Same parent, often the same hour. The product’s value is continuity: today’s rash is still that rash tonight; Meera’s knees accrue over months.

**Where it is used.** In the caregiver’s pocket, mostly as WhatsApp (later voice and Hindi / other Indian languages so grandparents can talk to it). India first — 108, Crocin, Dolo, joint families — then any home that shares care. The same companion sits in front of a family clinic or hospital: the family keeps context between visits; the doctor gets a one-page “what changed” instead of a blank intake form. School nurse lines and insurer triage are the same loop with different uniforms.

**Why this, not ChatGPT.** A general chat has no household, no episodes, and no hard stops. You re-explain who “he” is, paste last night’s temperature, and hope it remembers. It will hedge on chest pain. The companion *is* the family chart. Members and open episodes persist. A follow-up attaches without being asked. If it is unsure who you mean, it asks once rather than writing a guess into the record. Emergency and doctor-handoff wording are product rules, not model prose. The model talks; the product decides when talk is no longer enough.

**North star.** Home care stays in the thread. An opt-in “how is he today?” check-in updates the episode — silence is not recovery. For minor things that still need a human, the case is already in a partner GP/hospital queue, with a summary the clinician can trust (symptoms, timeline, what is still unknown). The family sees requested / accepted / done. No separate Practo booking, no retelling at reception. True emergencies skip the queue and go to 108. If the hospital rail is down, the product says so and tells you what to do next — it never pretends a transfer happened.

Labs, wearables, and a curated knowledge base plug in later. Memory and a real clinician rail come first; more integrations are not the product.

**Boundaries.** It intakes, monitors, and coordinates. It does not independently prescribe. Adults own their records; living in the same house is not a free pass to Meera’s chart. Inspect, correct, export, delete. Clinicians stay in the loop on the escalation policy.

Not a diagnostic engine. A long-lived front door to care — and two doors the model is not allowed to invent.
